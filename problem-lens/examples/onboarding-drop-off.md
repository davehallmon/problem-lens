# Example: Onboarding Drop-Off

## Input

Problem: Our onboarding drop-off is 60% at step 3.
Background: B2B SaaS, 200 signups per week, no user interviews yet.

## Output

Summary: The drop-off may be a symptom, not the problem. Find where in step 3 users quit before you change the flow. The simplest test uses data you already have.

| Priority | Solution | Lens Tags | When To Use This Lens | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Watch five users complete step 3 | Root Cause Analysis, Design Thinking | The symptom is visible but the cause is not | Direct observation finds the real cause faster than guessing | Small sample may not represent all users |
| 2 | Move step 3 to after first value | First Principles, PDCA | Inherited assumptions block progress | Users give effort after they see a payoff, so the step may be in the wrong place | Delays setup and may create later friction |
| 3 | Offer a skip option with a sensible default | TRIZ / TIPS | The problem hides a trade-off to break | Removes the clash between data you need and effort users will give | Skips may reduce data quality |
| 4 | Test a shorter step 3 on half of new signups for two weeks | PDCA, Divergent Thinking | The solution is a hypothesis, not a plan | 200 signups a week gives a fast read on a small change | Two weeks may be too short for a clear result |
| 5 | Ask sales and support what new accounts say about step 3 | Six Thinking Hats | One view dominates and others are missing | Teams who talk to customers see feelings and risks the product view misses | Secondhand reports may be filtered or stale |
| Anchor | Check analytics for the field where users quit | Occam's Razor | The solution is buried under complexity | Data you already have may name the cause in an hour | Field-level tracking may not exist yet |

Ranked by: Coverage (default). Other options: Impact vs. Effort, Confidence vs. Reversibility, ICE, RICE, Eisenhower, Regret Minimization, Optionality, Cost of Delay.
