**Summary**
The plan-versus-experiment fight is a symptom: the root is that no one knows why nurses leave, so both camps are guessing at a fix. Assuming a unit of about 40 nurses, 28% turnover means about 11 exits a year, which is too few to trust exit-interview themes alone and too slow for turnover itself to judge a short test. Once you know which causes have known fixes, both camps can be right: plan for those causes and test the rest.

**What's Likely Going On**
- First-year churn: new nurses leave before they settle in (guess). Check: the share of the last 2–3 years of exits with under 12 months on the unit.
- A local driver such as one shift, staffing level, or manager (guess). Check: whether exits cluster by shift or leader, and whether sister units at the same hospital run well below 28%.
- Market pull: nurses leave for better pay or schedules elsewhere (guess). Check: where leavers went (another hospital or an agency for the same work, or out of bedside nursing).

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1, split past exits by tenure, shift, and leader, because it tells the three explanations apart with data you already hold.
- Switch to Row 3 if most leavers took the same job elsewhere for more pay or a better schedule.

| 3 | Compare your pay, ratios, and schedule options with the two or three employers your leavers joined. | First Principles | Both camps assume the fix is a new program, but a nurse stays when this job beats her next offer. | If the gap is pay or schedule, no retention program will close it. | Pay changes may sit outside the unit's control and need system approval. |

**Questions That Would Change the Pick**
1. How many nurses work on the unit? If it is well over 100, one year of exit data is enough and Row 1 gets faster; if it is under 25, the data is too thin, and Row 2 moves to first.
2. Do sister units run near 28% too? If so, the cause is likely system-wide (pay, policy), and Row 3 becomes first.
3. Has senior leadership set a date or budget for a plan? If a written plan is due soon, Row 6 moves up to run beside Row 1.
