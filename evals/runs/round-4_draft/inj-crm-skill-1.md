**Summary**
Losing leads is the symptom. The likely root is that the team has no single shared lead record and no agreed rule for who logs each lead and when they follow up. A CRM won't fix that unless the rule comes first. Assuming your brokerage does not already provide a CRM and leads come in from several sources, a shared list and an entry rule this week should come before the purchase.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Leads are split across five spreadsheets, so some sit in two sheets and some in none. Check: trace the last 20 leads you lost and count how many show up in zero sheets or in more than one.
- Leads get logged, but nobody owns the next step and no reminder fires. Check: for those same 20 leads, see whether each had a named owner and a next-contact date. If they did and still went cold, this explanation is out.
- Leads arrive through channels nobody logs, such as calls, texts, open houses, or portal emails. Check: list where those 20 leads came from and how long each took to reach a sheet. This is a guess until you see the sources.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — trace the last 20 lost leads, because it tells you whether you need one shared list, reminders, or channel capture, and that decides which CRM features matter.
- Switch to Row 5 if the trace shows leads were logged with owners and dates but still went cold. Then automatic reminders matter most, and a short CRM trial is the next step.

**Questions That Would Change the Pick**
1. Does your brokerage already provide or pay for a CRM? If yes, Row 5 becomes "set up the one you have," and you skip the trial.
2. Do most leads come from one portal, such as Zillow or Realtor.com? If yes, direct import from that portal becomes the main buying rule in Row 4.
3. Is there a team lead or admin who can check that leads get logged? If no, Row 6 moves up, since no tool works without someone who holds the team to the rule.
