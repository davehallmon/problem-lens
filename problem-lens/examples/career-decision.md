# Example: Career Decision

## Input

Problem: Should I leave my stable job to join a startup?
Background: 8 years experience, two kids, six months of savings.

## Output

Summary: The choice turns on runway and on why you want to leave, not on which job pays more now. Test startup life before you commit. Six months of savings is thin for two kids.

| Priority | Solution | Lens Tags | When To Use This Lens | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do a two-week trial project with the startup | PDCA, Design Thinking | The solution is a hypothesis, not a plan | Real exposure beats guesses about the job | Startup may not allow it |
| 2 | List why you want to leave, then ask "why" for each | Root Cause Analysis | The symptom is visible but the cause is not | The real cause may be fixable without leaving | May talk you out of a good move |
| 3 | Negotiate a part-time or contract role first | TRIZ / TIPS | The problem hides a trade-off to break | Breaks the clash between stability and upside | Startup may see it as low commitment |
| 4 | Save to 12 months of expenses before deciding | First Principles | Inherited assumptions block progress | Runway, not the job title, sets your real risk | Delays the decision by months |
| 5 | Ask five people who made the jump | Six Thinking Hats, CPS | One view dominates and others are missing | People outside your current job see risks and benefits you can't | Survivorship bias skews the sample |
| Anchor | Write down which choice you will regret least in ten years | Occam's Razor | The solution is buried under complexity | One clear question cuts through the noise | You may rationalize the answer |

Ranked by: Coverage (default). Other options: Impact vs. Effort, Confidence vs. Reversibility, ICE, RICE, Eisenhower, Regret Minimization, Optionality, Cost of Delay.
