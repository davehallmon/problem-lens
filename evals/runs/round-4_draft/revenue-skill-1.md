**1. Summary**

Flat revenue is a symptom. The likely root is that new work depends on referrals and the founders' network, with no system to find buyers. Assuming headcount and rates have not changed much in the last year, and that most new clients come from referrals.

**2. What's Likely Going On**

- New-client flow has dried up and existing clients carry the firm — check: count of new clients and their revenue per quarter for the last six quarters. A drop points here.
- The team is at capacity, so more demand can't become more revenue — check: billable utilization and headcount by quarter. Utilization above about 85% with flat headcount points here. (Guess: common in 40-person service firms.)
- New wins are real but offset by lost or shrinking accounts — check: revenue from clients held a year ago, this year vs. last year. A drop here with steady new-client revenue points here.

**3. Recommendation**

- Do this first: Row 1 — split the last six quarters of revenue into new clients, growth or decline in existing clients, lost clients, and rate, because it shows which of the three explanations is true before you spend money.
- Switch to Row 4 if utilization is above about 85% with flat headcount, because then capacity caps revenue and price is the fastest lever.
