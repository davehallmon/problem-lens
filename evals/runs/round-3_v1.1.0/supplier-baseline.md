# Supplier Bankruptcy: Analysis and Response

## The real problem

The real problem isn't running out of stock in 4 days. It's the gap between when you run out and when new stock can arrive. You've never ordered from either backup supplier, so their lead time is unknown. For kitchenware it's probably more than 4 days, and possibly weeks if anything is imported. Plan around three questions:

1. **How long is the gap?** That's backup lead time minus your real runway.
2. **How much revenue and customer goodwill can you protect during it?**
3. **What can you recover from the bankrupt supplier?**

## Challenge the "4 days" figure first

"4 days of stock" is almost certainly an average, and an average hides the useful detail.

- **Runway differs by SKU.** Your top sellers may run out in 1–2 days, and slow movers may last a month. Pull a SKU-level report today showing units on hand ÷ average daily units sold.
- **Not every SKU came from this supplier.** Anything from other vendors isn't at risk.
- **Orders don't all deplete stock evenly.** Multi-item orders and bundles can drain one component faster than the rest.

That report decides almost everything below, so do it in the first hour.

## What to do today

**1. Contact the bankrupt supplier's administrator, trustee or receiver**
- Ask about your open POs, any goods already in transit, and any deposits or prepayments you've made.
- Ask whether their finished inventory is for sale. Administrators often want to sell stock fast, sometimes at a discount, and that can be your quickest source.
- Find out who actually made the products. If the supplier was a distributor, you may be able to buy from the factory directly.

**2. Contact both backup suppliers today, in parallel**
- Ask for lead time, minimum order quantities, payment terms for a new account (expect prepayment), and whether they can fill a small rush order.
- Ask for samples, or inspect their stock. Taking on an untested supplier under pressure is how quality problems get through.
- Consider splitting your first orders across both. You don't know yet which one is reliable, and you've just learned what depending on a single supplier costs.

**3. Stretch the runway you have**
- **Cut paid acquisition now** on SKUs that will run out. Paying to win orders you can't fill is the worst outcome.
- **Rank your remaining stock**: repeat customers and high-margin orders first, then wholesale or bulk orders you can delay.
- **Switch low-stock SKUs to pre-order or backorder** with an honest ship date instead of letting them sell out. You keep the sale and the customer, and you buy time.
- **Steer demand** toward SKUs you have plenty of (homepage, email, substitutes on product pages).

**4. Talk to customers before they notice**
- Don't stay silent and then cancel orders in bulk. A short, honest delay notice ("shipping in 10–14 days") loses far fewer customers than surprise cancellations.
- Prepare customer service with a script and a clear policy on refunds and substitutions.

## The next 1–3 weeks

- **Cash:** prepaying a new supplier while revenue drops is a cash squeeze. Model it now. You may need a short credit line or a stop on discretionary spending.
- **Quality control:** check the first shipment from any new supplier before it goes out to customers.
- **Listings:** if product specs, materials or packaging change, update your product pages. In kitchenware, food-contact materials and safety claims matter.
- **Staff:** with 12 people, give one person sole ownership of supply recovery and one of customer communications, so this doesn't spread across the whole team.

## Questions that change the plan

- Are the products generic (pans, utensils) or custom/private-label? Generic products can be re-sourced in days. Custom molds or branded packaging can take months, and in that case buying from the administrator or going direct to the factory becomes your main plan, not an extra.
- Do you have money tied up with the bankrupt supplier? If so, file your creditor claim promptly, but don't count on getting much back.
- Do any marketplace channels (Amazon, etc.) penalize stockouts or late shipments? If so, protect those listings first.

## Bottom line

You have 4 days of stock, but runway isn't your problem. The gap after it is. Today, get SKU-level runway, call the administrator and both backups, stop advertising what you can't ship, and move scarce items to pre-order with honest dates. Once you know the backup lead times, you'll know whether this is a one-week inconvenience or a month-long fight to keep customers.
