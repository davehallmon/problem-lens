**Summary**
A second receptionist treats the symptom (long waits) with a full-time cost for a two-hour daily spike. The likely root is that the phone is the only way to book, so all routine calls stack up in the 8–10am window. Assuming you have one receptionist now who also checks patients in, and your phone system can show call counts by hour.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Peak overload: more calls arrive between 8 and 10am than one person can answer at once — check: missed or abandoned calls by hour; if most fall between 8 and 10 and few after, this is a peak problem, not an all-day one.
- Phone-only booking: routine booking, rescheduling, and confirmation calls fill the line — check: tally the reason for each call for one week; if more than half are book, move, or confirm, a non-phone channel would cut the load.
- Split attention (guess): the receptionist is checking in patients who arrive at 8am and can't pick up — check: count rings that go unanswered while someone stands at the desk.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — log a week of calls by hour and by reason, because it tells you which of the three explanations is true before you commit to a salary.
- Switch to Row 3 if you already know most calls are booking or rescheduling; skip the log and start there.
