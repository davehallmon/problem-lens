**Summary.** Low teen visits are the symptom. The likely root is that the branch stopped fitting how teens spend their after-school time, and no one knows why because no one has asked them. Assuming the drop is branch-wide and not caused by a closure or renovation, the first job is to find out what changed before spending the one pilot budget.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Something changed access in 2022, such as shorter hours, a new rule, a bus route, or a new school schedule — check: compare branch hours and rule changes since 2022 with the nearest school's dismissal time and transit; if the drop is bunched in certain hours or days, this explanation leads.
- Teens moved to another place, such as a rec center, a café, or home and phones (guess) — check: ask teens and school staff where students go after school now; if they name one place, this explanation leads.
- The branch feels unwelcoming to teens: rules on noise, food, or groups, staff manner, or no space of their own (guess) — check: ask the teens who still come and those who stopped what they dislike; if both name the same rules or space, this explanation leads.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — talk with 15–20 teens, at the branch and at the nearest school, because it is the only check that tests all three explanations at once.
- Switch to Row 3 if you can't reach enough teens within two weeks; the visit records can still show whether the drop tracks a 2022 change.
