**Summary**
Flat revenue is a symptom; the likely root is that new business arrives only by referral and no one owns finding new clients. Assuming most of your 40 staff bill client hours and you have at least two years of invoices by client, the first job is to learn whether you are short on clients, short on capacity, or losing clients as fast as you win them. Assuming no one has made that split yet, start there.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Too few new clients: referrals have slowed and nothing replaces them (guess) — check: count of new clients and proposals sent per quarter, this year vs. last year.
- Churn hides growth: new wins only replace clients who left or spent less — check: split each quarter's revenue into new, returning, and lost clients; if new revenue held steady but lost revenue grew, this is it.
- Capacity ceiling: staff are fully booked, so revenue cannot rise without more people or higher rates — check: billable utilization per quarter and any work turned down; near-full use with work turned away points here.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — split revenue by new, returning, and lost clients, because the answer tells you which of the three explanations is true before you spend on any fix.
- Switch to Row 5 if utilization is near full and you are turning work away.

3. Do you already know where new clients came from over the past two years? If you do, Row 1 shrinks to a quick check and Row 6 can start now on the channel that dried up.
