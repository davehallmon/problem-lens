# Example: Business Strategy

## Input

Problem: Revenue is flat for three quarters.
Background: B2B services firm, 40 staff, no marketing team.

## Output

Summary: Flat revenue may be a pipeline problem, a pricing problem, or a retention problem. Diagnose before you spend. The simplest test is to check the win rate and deal size you already track.

| Priority | Solution | Lens Tags | When To Use This Lens | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call ten lost deals and ten renewals | Root Cause Analysis, Design Thinking | The symptom is visible but the cause is not | Direct evidence beats internal debate | Small sample may mislead |
| 2 | Run three small growth probes at once instead of one big plan | Cynefin | The user applies the wrong method to the problem type | Flat revenue with no clear cause is a complex problem, and probes show what works | Probes split a small team's attention |
| 3 | Package a productized offer | TRIZ / TIPS, CPS | The problem hides a trade-off to break | A standard offer breaks the clash between custom work and growth without new headcount | May alienate custom clients |
| 4 | Price new deals by value, not hours | First Principles | Inherited assumptions block progress | Hourly pricing caps revenue per person, so headcount sets the ceiling | May slow new deals |
| 5 | Hire one part-time marketer for a 90-day test | PDCA, Solution Space Exploration | The solution is a hypothesis, not a plan | A fixed test shows whether demand is the bottleneck | Adds cost before diagnosis |
| Anchor | Compare win rate and average deal size by quarter | Occam's Razor | The solution is buried under complexity | Two numbers may show whether pipeline or price slipped | Records may be incomplete |

Ranked by: Coverage (default). Other options: Impact vs. Effort, Confidence vs. Reversibility, ICE, RICE, Eisenhower, Regret Minimization, Optionality, Cost of Delay.
