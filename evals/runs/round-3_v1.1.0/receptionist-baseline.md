## Short answer

Probably not as the first move. A second receptionist would help with peak-hour capacity, but it's the most expensive fix and it may be aimed at the wrong problem. Your own background suggests the real issue is **demand that bunches into two hours, made worse by having only one channel for it**, not a general shortage of front-desk staff.

## What the background tells you

- **Calls peak 8–10am.** The problem sits in a two-hour window. A full-time hire gives you about 40 hours a week of capacity, and you're short for maybe 10 of them. For the other ~30 hours the new person is mostly doing whatever else the front desk does.
- **No online booking.** Every booking, reschedule, and "what time is my appointment?" has to come in by phone. A large share of that morning rush is probably routine traffic that doesn't need a human.
- **3 dentists.** One receptionist covering three providers' schedules, check-ins, check-outs, insurance, and phones is a heavy load. The person at the desk is likely being pulled off the phone by patients standing in front of them, which is exactly when hold times grow.

## What you don't know yet (and should find out before deciding)

1. **What the calls are about.** Track one or two weeks of morning calls by type: new booking, reschedule/cancel, confirmation, billing/insurance, clinical question, emergency. If 40–60% are booking or confirmation calls, self-service could remove them.
2. **What "wait time" means.** Are patients on hold, getting a busy signal, or going to voicemail and waiting for a callback? Each one points to a different fix.
3. **What the current receptionist does from 8–10am.** If they're also checking in the first patients of the day, the phones lose by default.
4. **How many calls are abandoned.** Abandoned calls cost you new patients, not just goodwill. Your phone system can probably report this.

## Options, roughly from cheapest to most expensive

| Option | What it fixes | Cost / effort |
|---|---|---|
| Online booking and rescheduling (most practice-management systems have it, or there are add-ons) | Takes routine booking calls out of the phone queue entirely | Low to moderate; patients need a little time to adopt it |
| Automated text/email reminders with confirm/reschedule links | Cuts "confirming my appointment" calls and no-shows | Low |
| Phone-tree or voicemail triage ("press 1 for emergencies…") plus a guaranteed callback window | Gets urgent calls through; turns waiting on hold into a scheduled callback | Low |
| Separate phone from front desk during 8–10am (an assistant or office manager takes check-ins, or the receptionist takes only calls) | Ends the in-person vs. phone conflict at peak | Low; it's a scheduling change |
| Part-time or split-shift help covering the peak (e.g., 7:30–11:30) | Adds staff right when you need it | Moderate |
| Remote or outsourced overflow answering for peak hours | Flexible capacity without a hire | Moderate, ongoing |
| Full-time second receptionist | Capacity at all hours, coverage for vacations and sick days | Highest; adds a fixed cost every year |

## Recommendation

1. **Measure first (1–2 weeks):** call volume by hour, call types, hold times, abandonment rate.
2. **Put in the cheap fixes now:** online booking, automated reminders with self-service rescheduling, and a call-handling rule for 8–10am so the phone person isn't also running check-in.
3. **Re-measure after 4–6 weeks.** If wait times are still a problem, add peak-hour help (part-time or overflow service) before committing to a full-time salary.
4. **Hire full-time only if** the data shows sustained overload outside the morning peak, or the desk is genuinely overloaded with non-phone work (insurance, billing, treatment coordination) that a second person would absorb.

## Where a second hire *is* the right call

To be fair to the original plan: if your practice is growing, if the current receptionist is burning out, if you have no coverage when they're sick or on vacation, or if your patients skew older and won't use online tools, a second person may still be justified. In that case the hire would be about resilience and workload, not wait times. Make that case on its own terms, and still add online booking, because without it the new hire inherits the same morning bottleneck.

## The key question to answer

**Is this a capacity problem or a channel-and-timing problem?** With a two-hour peak and no self-service option, the evidence leans toward channel and timing. A week of call logs will tell you which it is.
