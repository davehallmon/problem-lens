**Summary.** The real question is likely what you want that your current job does not give you, and whether this startup can pay you reliably while you get it. Assuming you have a specific startup in mind, it offers a cash salary, and your pay keeps the household running, the decision turns on the startup's cash runway and the size of any pay cut. With six months of savings and two kids, an unpaid or equity-only role is out; a paid role at a startup with little runway is close to the same risk.

**What's Likely Going On**
- You have outgrown your current job (stalled growth, pay, or interest), and the startup is one exit among several — check: would a new role, raise, or team move where you are now remove the wish to leave?
- This startup pulls you on its own merits (the team, the product, the equity) — check: would you still leave for a different startup in the same field? If not, the pull is this company, and its finances matter most.
- The job is fine and the fear of missing out is doing the talking (a guess) — check: can you name one concrete thing you would do at the startup in month one that you cannot do now?

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — write down why you want to leave, because the answer decides whether a startup is the fix at all.
- Switch to Row 5 if the reason turns out to be your current job, not this startup.

**Questions That Would Change the Pick**
1. Is your income the only income in the household? If there is a second steady income, six months of savings goes further, and Row 3 drops in priority.
2. How much cash runway and funding does the startup have? If it has well over a year of runway and a recent round, Row 2 is settled and Row 4 becomes the first move.
3. How large is the pay cut, if any? A cut that forces you to draw on savings each month shrinks your buffer, and moves Row 4 to the top.
