Assuming you have exit data by name, date, shift, and manager, and assuming 28% is well above your hospital's other units (a guess), the first step is to find out which cause dominates before choosing between "plan" and "try."

**What's Likely Going On**
- First-year nurses leave at a far higher rate than the rest (guess) — check: split the last 12–24 months of departures by tenure; if more than half left within their first year, this is the main driver.
- One manager, shift, or staffing pattern drives most exits (guess) — check: split departures by manager, shift, and overtime hours; if exits cluster on one shift or under one leader, it is local, not unit-wide.
- Nurses leave for better pay or terms elsewhere, not because of the unit (guess) — check: where leavers went; if most took the same role at another hospital or agency, pay and terms lead; if they left bedside nursing, workload or burnout leads.
