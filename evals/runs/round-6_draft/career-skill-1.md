**Summary.** "Stay or go" is a symptom question: the likely root is either something your current job no longer gives you or a pull toward this specific startup, and those call for different moves. Assuming you have a specific offer, the startup pays less than your current job, and your income covers most of the household costs, the real test is whether this startup can pay your family's monthly costs and stay funded long enough. With two kids and six months of savings, the cushion covers one bad outcome, not a long pay cut.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Your current job has stalled (pay, growth, or interest) and the startup is the first exit that showed up — check: would you take a stable job with the same role and pay as the startup offers? If yes, the problem is your job, not a lack of startup.
- This startup itself is the draw (the work, the people, the mission) — check: would you still join if the equity turned out to be worth zero? If yes, the pull is real and not just a bet on a payout.
- Guess: the main risk is the startup's cash, not your choice — check: how many months of cash it has and when it last raised. Under about 12 months means a funding round or cuts could come before you settle in.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — write down why you want to leave, because the answer decides whether you need this startup or just a different job.
- Switch to Row 4 if your reasons point at your current job rather than at this startup.

**Questions That Would Change the Pick**
1. Is there a second income in your household? If it covers the basics, six months of savings stretches further, Row 3's floor relaxes, and the trial in Row 6 could move up.
2. Does the offer's salary already cover your monthly costs? If yes, Row 3 is settled, and Row 2's cash check becomes the main gate.
3. Does the startup provide health coverage for your family? If not, that cost raises the floor in Row 3 and may rule the offer out on its own.
