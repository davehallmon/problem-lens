## Lenses

### Creative Problem Solving (CPS)
A staged group process, from Osborn and Parnes, that moves from goal to ideas to plan.
Link: https://www.creativeeducationfoundation.org/creative-problem-solving/

### Divergent Thinking
Generating many different ideas before judging any of them.
Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergent_thinking

### Solution Space Exploration
Listing the other kinds of fix that could reach the same goal before committing to one.
Link: none (defined by this Skill, adapted from design practice)

### TRIZ / TIPS
Genrich Altshuller's method for solving problems by resolving contradictions with patterns from past inventions.
Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRIZ

### Root Cause Analysis
Tools such as 5 Whys and Fishbone diagrams that trace a symptom back to its cause.
Link: https://asq.org/quality-resources/root-cause-analysis

### Cynefin Framework
Dave Snowden's framework for matching your method to the type of problem: clear, complicated, complex, or chaotic.
Link: https://thecynefin.co/about-us/about-cynefin-framework/

### Six Thinking Hats
Edward de Bono's method for looking at a problem from six separate views, one at a time.
Link: https://www.debonogroup.com/services/core-programs/six-thinking-hats/

### First Principles
Reasoning from basic truths instead of from how things are usually done.
Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_principle

### Occam's Razor
Prefer the explanation that needs the fewest assumptions.
Link: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Occams-razor

### Design Thinking
A human-centered process: understand the people you serve, then prototype and test.
Link: https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/what-is-design-thinking

### PDCA (Plan–Do–Check–Act)
A four-step loop for testing a change in small steps.
Link: https://asq.org/quality-resources/pdca-cycle

### OODA Loop
John Boyd's cycle of observe, orient, decide, act, built for fast-changing situations.
Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OODA_loop

## Ranking Frameworks

### Coverage
Pick one solution from each of five lens families, then order by expected effect on the root problem.
Link: none (defined by this Skill)

### Impact vs. Effort
Prefer moves with high impact and low effort.
Link: none (common practice, no single source)

### Confidence vs. Reversibility
Move fast on decisions that are easy to undo, and slow down on decisions that are not. Based on Amazon's Type 1 and Type 2 decisions.
Link: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312516530910/d168744dex991.htm

### ICE (Impact, Confidence, Ease)
Score each option 1–10 on impact, confidence, and ease, then compare.
Link: none (common practice, no single source)

### RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort)
Intercom's score: reach times impact times confidence, divided by effort.
Link: https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/

### Eisenhower (Urgent vs. Important)
Sort tasks by urgency and importance: do, schedule, delegate, or drop.
Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_management

### Regret Minimization
Picture yourself at 80 and pick the choice you would regret least. Jeff Bezos describes it in his own words.
Link: https://achievement.org/achiever/jeffrey-p-bezos/

### Optionality
Prefer moves that keep more future choices open.
Link: none (defined by this Skill)

### Cost of Delay
Put a value on waiting, so urgency and value can be compared directly.
Link: https://blackswanfarming.com/cost-of-delay/
