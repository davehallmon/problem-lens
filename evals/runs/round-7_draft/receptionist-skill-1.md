**Summary.** The hire may be the wrong call. Calls peak from 8 to 10am, so a second full-time receptionist pays for about six hours a day outside the problem window. The likely root is too many calls packed into two hours, made worse by having no online booking. Assuming wait times are fine for the rest of the day, which you have not confirmed yet.

**What's Likely Going On**
- **Peak overload:** one person can't keep up with the 8–10am rush but copes the rest of the day. Check: hold times and dropped calls by hour from your phone system. If the long waits fall only between 8 and 10am, this is it.
- **Calls that don't need a person:** with no online booking, much of the volume is booking, rescheduling, and confirming visits. Check: have the front desk tag each call by reason for one week. If most calls are routine booking, this is it.
- **Receptionist pulled off the phone (guess):** check-ins, payments, and insurance work at the desk take the receptionist away from calls. Check: whether hold times jump when patients arrive or leave, even outside the peak.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1, pull two weeks of call logs and tag call reasons, because it shows whether you have a two-hour problem or an all-day one before you commit to a salary.
- Switch to Row 5 if the logs show long holds and dropped calls all day, not just from 8 to 10am.
