**Summary.** A new idea at every meeting is a symptom; the likely root is that the board has no shared facts on why donations fell and no rule for how it decides. Assuming donations make up most of the $400k budget, the 15% drop is roughly a $40k–$60k gap, which is big enough that the board needs one plan soon. Assuming you have an executive director (ED) but no one whose job is fundraising, any plan must also name who will carry it out.

**What's Likely Going On**
- No decision rule: meetings end in talk, not a vote, so the next idea always replaces the last — check: read the minutes from the last three or four meetings and see whether any motion was made or vote taken on a plan.
- No shared facts: members are guessing at the cause of the drop, so each pitches a fix for a different cause (guess) — check: pull gifts by source for the last three years; if no one on the board can say which source fell, this is it.
- No owner: ideas die between meetings because, without a development director, no one is assigned to carry them out — check: see whether any past idea was given to a named person with a date, and what happened to it.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — pull three years of gifts by source and trace the 15% drop to its cause before the next meeting, because the board can't agree on a fix while each member guesses at a different cause.
- Switch to Row 2 if the board already knows where the money was lost and the minutes show no vote was ever taken.
