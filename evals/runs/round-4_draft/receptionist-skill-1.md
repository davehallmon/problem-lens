**Summary**
A second full-time receptionist may fix a two-hour problem with an eight-hour cost. Assuming you have one receptionist who handles phones and front-desk check-in, the likely root is the 8–10am call peak hitting at the same time as the first patients arrive, plus calls that online booking would remove. Find out which one it is before you hire.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Peak overload: more calls come in from 8–10am than one person can answer, even with nothing else to do — check: phone logs by hour showing hold time and dropped calls, compared with the rest of the day.
- Desk conflict: the receptionist is checking in patients, taking payments, or handling forms when the calls come — check: watch the desk for two mornings and note whether hold times spike when patients are at the counter.
- Avoidable calls (guess): a large share of calls are booking, rescheduling, or confirming, which patients could do themselves — check: tally the reason for each call for one week.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — log call times and reasons for one week, because it tells you which of the three causes you have before you commit to a salary.
- Switch to Row 5 if the logs show the peak overloads the line even when the receptionist does nothing but answer phones, and most calls need a person.

**Questions That Would Change the Pick**
1. How many people handle phones now? If you already have two, the anchor (Row 2) is weaker and online booking (Row 4) moves up, since more hands have not fixed it.
2. Can your phone system already report hold times and dropped calls by hour? If yes, you can skip most of the week of logging and act on Row 2 or Row 5 within days.
3. Do patients complain about long holds or about calls going to voicemail? Voicemail complaints point to the callback option (Row 3) as the first fix.
