**Summary.** Assuming you have a firm offer, you earn most of your household's income, and six months of savings covers full family costs with no pay coming in. The likely root is not "startup or not". You need to know whether you are being pushed out of your job or pulled toward this startup, and whether your family can absorb the startup failing within a year.

**What's Likely Going On**
- The current job is the push (stalled growth, pay, boredom) — check: would a raise, promotion, or new team at your current employer remove most of the urge to leave?
- This startup is the pull (mission, equity, faster growth) — check: would you still take this role if it paid the same as now and had no equity?
- The real risk is the startup's finances, not your choice (a guess until you see numbers) — check: how many months of cash it has, when it last raised money, and whether revenue is growing.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — run the push-or-pull test on yourself, because the answer decides whether the startup or your current job is the thing to fix.
- Switch to Row 2 if you already know the startup itself is the pull; then its runway becomes the deciding fact.

**Questions That Would Change the Pick**
1. Does your partner have income that covers core costs? If yes, the risk drops and Row 5 (negotiate) moves up. If no, Row 3 (runway math) becomes the gate before anything else.
2. How many months of runway does the startup have? Under 12 months would move Row 6 (trial first) ahead of any move to quit.
3. How big is the pay cut in the offer? A large cut moves Row 5 (negotiate) to first.
