**Summary.** The bankruptcy is the trigger, but the root is single-sourcing: every order depends on one supplier, and neither backup is set up to ship yet. Assuming your stock mix matches your sales mix, 4 days at 300 orders a day is about 1,200 orders, but some top products will likely run out in a day or two while others last for weeks. Assuming the backups need more than 4 days to deliver, you face a gap and need to manage demand as well as find new supply.

**What's Likely Going On**
- The supplier has stopped for good, and paid orders or goods in transit are lost — check: ask the administrator or trustee whether any paid or in-transit orders will still ship.
- The supplier keeps trading under administration or gets sold, so some supply resumes — check: the administrator's notice on whether the business keeps operating and who handles open orders.
- Stock runs out unevenly, so the true deadline is set by a few best-selling products, not the 4-day average (a guess based on how online retail sales usually concentrate) — check: days of stock left per product, from current stock divided by recent daily sales.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — count days of stock left per product and rank them, because it tells you which products to source first and which can wait.
- Switch to Row 2 if you cannot get per-product counts within a few hours; call both backups about your best-known sellers instead.
