# Winning Back Lapsed Gym Members When Discounts Haven't Worked

## Start by questioning the premise

Three discount campaigns failed, and the obvious next step is a fourth offer with a different number on it. Don't do that. Three failures are telling you something: **price probably isn't why these people left.** Someone who quit because they stopped going, felt out of place, got injured, moved, or never saw results won't come back for 20% off. A cheaper version of something they had stopped using is still something they don't use.

Before planning the next move, figure out which of two things happened.

### 1. Did the campaigns fail, or did the emails fail?

Pull the numbers for each campaign: delivery rate, open rate, click rate and conversions.

- **Low opens (under about 15%) or a high bounce rate:** the offer was never tested. The message didn't reach people. Emails up to 18 months old decay, mass promotional sends get filtered into Promotions or spam, and "lapsed member discount" subject lines get ignored. Fix delivery before you judge the offer.
- **Decent opens but almost no clicks:** people saw the offer and didn't want it. That supports the "it's not about price" theory.
- **Clicks but no sign-ups:** the problem is what happens after the click, such as the landing page, the sign-up process or the terms.

These three cases call for very different fixes, so find out which one you're in first.

### 2. You probably have more than email addresses

"We only have their email addresses" is likely true for contact details but not for data. Most gym management systems store join date, cancellation date, visit history, membership type and sometimes a cancellation reason. That's enough to sort 1,200 people into groups that need different approaches:

| Segment | Likely reason they left | What might bring them back |
|---|---|---|
| Joined, visited a few times, faded out | Never built a habit, felt intimidated, didn't know what to do | Guided restart: a free session with a coach, a beginner small group |
| Regular for a long time, then stopped suddenly | Life event: new job, baby, injury, move | Personal check-in; flexible or paused membership options |
| Attended classes, then stopped | Schedule change, instructor left, class cancelled | News about specific classes and new times |
| Left recently (0 to 6 months) | Easiest to win back | Fast, personal outreach |
| Left 12 to 18 months ago | Many have moved, joined another gym or quit for good | Light-touch; accept low returns |

Even a rough split by tenure, recency and visit pattern will do better than sending everyone the same message.

## What to try instead

### Ask before you offer
Send a short, plain-text email from the owner or a named manager, not a designed promotional email. Something like: *"You were a member here until last spring. I'd really like to know what made you stop coming. Just hit reply. One line is fine."* Response rates are usually low, but even 30 to 60 replies will tell you more than three campaigns did. As a fallback, link a one-question survey with 5 to 6 reasons to pick from.

This also works as win-back outreach on its own. Being asked feels very different from being sold to.

### Offer something other than a lower price
Match the offer to the likely reason they left:
- **Fresh-start program:** a 4-week small-group cohort for returning members with a fixed start date, a coach and a group. This targets the most common real reason people quit, which is that they lost the habit and felt alone. A start date gives people urgency without a discount.
- **Bring a friend:** a free month for them plus a friend. Having a workout partner deals with motivation directly.
- **Flexible membership:** if you only sell all-or-nothing monthly plans, a class pack, off-peak plan or pause option may fit someone whose life changed.
- **"What's new":** new equipment, classes, hours or staff. This gives people a reason to come back that doesn't depend on a deal.

### Fix the delivery
- Send in small batches from a real person's address, in plain text.
- Clean the list first: remove hard bounces and run it through a verification tool.
- Write subject lines that sound like a person, such as "Quick question from [Name] at [Gym]", not "Come back and save 30%!"

### Use the neighborhood
Being a neighborhood gym is an advantage over chains. Email isn't your only channel. Upload the list to Facebook and Instagram as a custom audience to show local ads to people you can't reach by email. Host community events like a free outdoor class or a 5K. Ask current members to invite people they used to train with.

## The harder question

Ask whether win-back is the best use of your effort at all. 1,200 lapsed members in 18 months is a lot for a neighborhood gym. If that's a large share of your membership, the bigger problem is retention, not reactivation. Whatever you learn about why people left will probably apply to the members you have now. Their early warning sign is dropping visit frequency in the first 90 days, and you can see it coming while you can still act.

A realistic outcome: a well-run, segmented, personal win-back program often brings back a few percent of a lapsed list, so roughly 25 to 60 people here. That's worth doing. But the bigger result may be learning why people leave and stopping the next 1,200.

## Suggested sequence

1. **This week:** Review the metrics from the three past campaigns to find out whether the problem was delivery, the offer or what happened after the click.
2. **Week 2:** Export the member data and split the list into 3 to 4 segments.
3. **Week 3:** Send the plain-text "why did you leave?" email to the most recent segment first.
4. **Week 5:** Build one non-price offer based on what people said, most likely a fresh-start cohort, and test it on one segment.
5. **Ongoing:** Apply what you learn to keeping current members.
