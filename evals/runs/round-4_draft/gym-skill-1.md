**Summary**
Three discount campaigns failing is a symptom. The likely root is that you don't yet know why these members left, and discounts only work if price was the reason. Assuming you can see basic email stats (opens, clicks, bounces) and that each campaign led with a price cut.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Price was not why they left. Schedule, a move, an injury, or lost habit is more common for gym lapses (guess) — check: replies to a one-question "why did you stop coming?" email. If few replies mention cost, discounts were aimed at the wrong cause.
- The emails never landed. Old addresses, spam folders, or a skimmed promo look (guess) — check: open and bounce rates from the three campaigns. Low opens with high bounces point here. Normal opens with near-zero clicks point back to the first explanation.
- Many are gone for good. They moved or joined another gym (guess) — check: what share of survey replies say "moved" or "joined elsewhere," and whether lapses from 12–18 months ago respond less than those from the last 6 months.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — pull the open, click, and bounce rates from the three campaigns, because it takes an hour and tells you whether the offers failed or were never seen.
- Switch to Row 6 if the survey shows most lapsed members moved away or joined another gym.

| 1 · Anchor | Pull open, click, and bounce rates from the three past campaigns. | Occam's Razor | "Nobody saw the emails" needs fewer assumptions than "nobody wanted the offer." | It sorts the failure into a delivery problem or a message problem before you spend more. | The stats may be missing or too thin to read if your email tool kept poor records. |
| 2 | Email all 1,200 one plain question: "What made you stop coming?" with four tap-to-answer choices plus "other." | Root Cause Analysis | You can see that people left, but not why. | The answers tell you which fix to build instead of guessing again. | Reply rates may be low, and those who reply may not match those who stay silent. |
| 3 | Split the list by when they lapsed, and test one new message on about 200 of the most recent lapsers before sending to the rest. | PDCA | Each new idea is a guess, and you have 1,200 contacts to test on in small batches. | Recent lapsers are most likely to return, so a small test shows signal fast without burning the whole list. | A batch of 200 may give too few returns to tell a real result from chance. |
| 4 | Have the owner or a coach the member knew send short, personal, plain-text notes with no offer attached. | Divergent Thinking | All three tries were price offers, so change who is asking instead of the deal. | A note from a person they knew reads as an invitation, not a promotion. | It takes staff time, and coaches may not remember or still know many of these members. |
| 5 | Offer a "first week back" plan: one booked check-in, a lighter routine, and a set time to come in. | Six Thinking Hats | Price thinking has dominated; the feelings view is missing — returning after months away can feel awkward. | It lowers the effort and embarrassment of walking back in, which a discount does not touch. | If lapses were about schedule or a move, this solves a problem they don't have. |
