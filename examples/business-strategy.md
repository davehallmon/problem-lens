# Example: Business Strategy

## Input

Problem: Revenue is flat for three quarters.
Background: B2B services firm, 40 staff, no marketing team.

## Output

Summary: Flat revenue may be a pipeline problem, a pricing problem, or a retention problem. Diagnose before you spend. The simplest test is to call ten lost deals.

| Priority | Solution | Lens Tags | When To Use This Lens | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call ten lost deals and ten renewals | Root Cause Analysis, Design Thinking | The cause is unknown | Direct evidence beats internal debate | Small sample may mislead |
| 2 | Raise prices 10% on new deals only | Occam's Razor, First Principles | Costs have risen | Price is the fastest lever to test | May slow new deals |
| 3 | Package a productized offer | CPS, TRIZ | Services are custom and slow | Standard offers scale without new headcount | May alienate custom clients |
| 4 | Hire one part-time marketer | Solution Space Exploration, PDCA | Demand is the bottleneck | Tests whether pipeline is the real issue | Adds cost before diagnosis |
| 5 | Launch a referral program for existing clients | Divergent Thinking, Six Thinking Hats | Retention is strong | Low-cost channel that uses current trust | Referrals may not scale |
| Anchor | Ask ten clients why they buy | Occam's Razor | The answer is one call away | One question may reset strategy | Clients may not say the truth |

Ranked by: Coverage (default). Other options: Impact vs. Effort, Confidence vs. Reversibility, ICE, RICE, Eisenhower, Regret Minimization, Optionality, Cost of Delay.
