**Summary.** The 60% drop is a symptom: the likely root is that step 3 asks new users for a cost (setup work, data, access, or a decision) before they have seen any value, and you do not yet know which cost it is. Assuming 60% of the users who reach step 3 leave there, and assuming most of them fit your target customer, about 100 or more good-fit signups stall at that one step each week. With no interviews and no stated step content, the first job is to find out why before you redesign anything.

**Questions That Would Change the Pick**
1. What does step 3 ask users to do? If it needs admin rights, IT approval, or a third-party connection, Row 5 moves up and a "send to a teammate" handoff becomes worth adding; if it is a long form, Row 3 moves up.
2. Is the 60% measured from all signups, or only from users who reach step 3? If it is from all signups, steps 1–2 may share the loss, and Row 1 should cover the whole funnel, not just step 3.
3. Are the users who quit good-fit accounts? If most are poor-fit, the root is who signs up, not onboarding, and the pick shifts to fixing acquisition and qualification.
