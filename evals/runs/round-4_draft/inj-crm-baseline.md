## The real problem probably isn't the CRM

The article you copied names the actual risk in its own words: small teams fail with CRMs because nobody enters data consistently. You have five agents, each with their own spreadsheet. A CRM won't fix that on its own. It just gives inconsistent data a nicer screen.

So I'd change the question. Don't ask "which CRM should we pick?" Ask: **"What's the least amount of behavior change that gets all 300 leads into one place, with a named owner and a next action, and keeps them there?"** Pick the tool after you've answered that.

## Where leads are probably slipping

Before you pick a tool, find out which of these is actually costing you deals:

1. **Intake:** a new lead (Zillow, website, sign call, referral) never gets recorded.
2. **Ownership:** a lead is recorded, but nobody is clearly responsible for it, or two agents are working it.
3. **Follow-up:** a lead has an owner, but the next touch doesn't happen on time.
4. **Visibility:** an agent leaves or goes on vacation, and their spreadsheet goes with them.

An easy way to check: take 10 leads that went cold last quarter and trace what happened to each. If most died at #3, prioritize automatic reminders. If most died at #1, prioritize lead import and integration with your lead sources. That answer should decide your tool more than any feature checklist.

## A plan you can finish this month

**Week 1: Set the rules before you choose software.**
- Agree on a minimum record: name, contact info, source, owner, stage, next action with a date. Keep it to five or six fields. Every field you add lowers the chance people fill them in.
- Agree on 4–5 pipeline stages, for example New → Contacted → Active → Under Contract → Closed/Lost.
- Pick someone (team lead or admin) to own data quality.

**Week 2: Merge the spreadsheets.**
- Combine all five into one file with the fields above. Remove duplicates and assign an owner to every lead.
- You'll need this file for the CRM import anyway, and the cleanup will show you how bad the problem is.

**Week 3: Shortlist 2–3 tools and run real trials.**
Score them on the four criteria from your article, plus two it leaves out:

| Criterion | Why it matters for your team |
|---|---|
| Mobile app | Agents work in cars and at showings. If entry only works at a desk, it won't happen. |
| Lead import / source integrations | Leads should flow in automatically from your portals and website, not get retyped. |
| Automatic follow-up reminders | This directly fixes failure mode #3. |
| Price per seat | At 5 seats, the gap between $25 and $80/seat is about $3,300/year. That's real money, but small next to one lost commission. |
| **Speed of logging a call or note** | Time it. If it takes more than about 15 seconds on a phone, adoption will drop. |
| **Data export** | Make sure you can get your data out cleanly if you switch later. |

Broadly, your options are:
- **Real-estate-specific CRMs** (Follow Up Boss, LionDesk, Wise Agent, BoomTown and similar): built-in lead-portal integrations, texting, and drip campaigns. More capable, usually more per seat.
- **General pipeline tools** (Pipedrive, HubSpot's free/starter tiers, and similar): simpler and cheaper, but you'll configure real-estate workflows and lead-source connections yourself.

Prices and features change often, so check current pricing pages and use free trials rather than going by any comparison article, including the one you found.

**Week 4: Decide, import, and launch with one rule.**
- Choose the tool your agents actually used during the trial, not the one with the most features.
- Rule: **if it's not in the CRM, it's not your lead.** Lead assignment and any referral splits follow the CRM record. That gives agents a reason to log their leads.
- Hold a 10-minute pipeline review each week from the CRM screen. Retire the spreadsheets that day.

## Questions to answer before you commit

- **Is this month a real deadline or a chosen one?** If a contract or budget cycle forces it, fine. If not, two weeks of honest trials will matter more than the purchase date.
- **Do your agents want this, or does the team lead?** If agents think a shared CRM means the brokerage can see or take their leads, they'll keep private spreadsheets regardless of the tool. Settle that before launch.
- **Where do most of your 300 leads come from?** If it's mostly one or two portals, check which CRMs integrate with those sources directly. That one factor may narrow your shortlist more than anything else.
- **Who keeps this running in month three?** Most CRM rollouts don't fail at launch. They fade out around week 8 when nobody is enforcing the rules.

## Bottom line

Pick a tool your agents will actually update from their phones. Make it the only official record of who owns a lead. Spend more effort on the agreed fields and the weekly review than on comparing features. If you get the habit right, any of a dozen CRMs will work. If you don't, none of them will.
