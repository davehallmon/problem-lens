**Summary**
Flat revenue is a symptom; the likely root is that nobody owns finding new work, so growth rests on referrals and repeat clients that have leveled off. Assuming you bill mostly by time, staff are not fully booked, and no single client is a large share of revenue. With 40 staff and no marketing team, hiring an agency or building a marketing team is a big fixed cost to take on before you know whether demand or capacity is the cap, so it is left off the list.

**What's Likely Going On**
- Pipeline gap: new-client wins have slowed, and they only just replace clients you lose — check: split each of the last six quarters into revenue from new clients, existing clients, and clients lost.
- Capacity cap: staff are booked full, so revenue can't grow without hires or higher rates — check: billable utilization by quarter; if it sits near 85% or higher, this is the cause (guess: less likely if teams have slack time).
- Price or scope erosion: same clients, smaller projects or deeper discounts — check: average rate and average project size over the same six quarters.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — split revenue and utilization by quarter, because it tells you which of the three causes is real before you spend anything.
- Switch to Row 5 if utilization is near or above 85%, since then price, not demand, is the lever.

3. Do you bill by the hour or by fixed fee? If you already sell fixed-fee work, Row 6 drops out and pricing tests in Row 5 apply to package prices instead.
