**Summary.** A new idea at every meeting is a symptom. The likely root is that the board has no way to decide and no one who owns fundraising, so the newest idea wins the room until the next meeting. Assuming donations are most of the $400k and the board, not staff, must write the plan.

**Recommendation.**
- Do this first: Row 1 — run one facilitated session that turns the ideas already raised into one plan, because the board lacks a shared process, not ideas.
- Switch to Row 4 if no board member has run a fundraising plan before, since then the debate is opinion against opinion and an expert draft will end it faster.

| Priority | Solution | Lens | Why This Lens | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hold one half-day session with a neutral facilitator: agree on the goal, list every idea raised so far, build out the top two, and leave with one plan and owners. | CPS | Nine people must agree on one plan and keep starting over. | A staged process stops new ideas from resetting the talk each time. | If the chair or a strong member runs it instead of a neutral person, it can turn into a push for their own idea. |
| 2 | Name a fundraising committee of three with power to pick the plan; the full board only votes yes or no on it. | First Principles | The board assumes all nine must shape the plan. | Three people can reach a choice that nine cannot, and the committee becomes the owner the missing director would have been. | Members left off the committee may block the vote or disengage. |
| 3 | Pull three years of donor records and find where the 15% went: lost donors, smaller gifts, or one large donor gone. | Root Cause Analysis | The drop is visible but its cause is not. | Each cause points to a different plan, so this tells the board which ideas to drop. | Records may be thin or messy without a development director to keep them. |
| 4 | Hire a part-time or contract fundraising consultant to draft the plan; the board edits and approves it. | Cynefin | The board treats an expert task as a matter of opinion. | A plan built on known practice gives the board one draft to react to instead of nine. | Fees cut into a budget already short, and the board may still reopen the plan. |
| 5 | Pick one idea, run it for 90 days with a dollar target, then keep, change, or drop it. | PDCA | The board cannot know which idea works without trying one. | A real result settles the debate better than another meeting. | One short test may fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the idea. |
| Anchor | The chair sets a decision date and a rule today: no new ideas after the next meeting, then a ranked vote on the ideas already on the table. | Occam's Razor | | Fixed rules end the loop at no cost. | The vote may pick a weak plan just to be done. |
