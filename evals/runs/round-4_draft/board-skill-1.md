**Summary.** A new idea at every meeting is a symptom. The likely root is that the board has no shared view of why donations fell and no process or owner to turn ideas into one decision. Assuming you have an executive director or treasurer who can pull donor records, and that the plan must be set before the next fiscal year starts.

**What's Likely Going On**
- No decision process or owner: meetings talk but never close — check: read the last six months of minutes for any motion, vote, or named owner on fundraising. If none, this is the main cause.
- No shared diagnosis: each member pitches a fix for a different guessed cause — check: split the 15% drop into lost donors, smaller gifts from the same donors, and lost large gifts or grants. If one bucket holds most of the drop, the board has been arguing without that fact.
- Guess: unspoken split on the board's own role — some members do not want to ask for money, so they favor ideas that don't need them to — check: board giving rate (how many of nine gave last year) and which past ideas required members to make asks.

**Recommendation**
- Do this first: Row 1 — sort the 15% drop by donor type before the next meeting, because the board can't agree on a fix while each member assumes a different cause.
- Switch to Row 2 if the minutes show no meeting has ever ended with a vote or an owner; then the process gap matters more than the data.
