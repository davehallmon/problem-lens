**Summary**

Low teen visits are the symptom; the likely root is that the branch does not know why teens left or what they want now, because staff have never asked them. A drop by half since 2022 is big enough that it may trace to one specific change, such as hours, a rule, or a new place teens go, but that is a guess. Assuming the branch keeps visit counts by age and the pilot budget covers one program for a few months, find the cause before you spend the one pilot.

**What's Likely Going On**

These are guesses; none is confirmed yet.

- Access changed: branch hours, bus routes, or school dismissal times no longer line up with the hours after school — check: compare branch hours and bus times with each nearby school's dismissal time, now and in 2022.
- Teens found somewhere else to be (a new rec center, a cafe, a school program, home or phone) — check: ask school staff and teens where students spend 3–6 p.m. now and when that started.
- Something at the branch pushed them out (a noise or food rule, a lost teen space, a staff or security change) — check: whether the monthly drop starts within a few months of a dated branch change.

**Recommendation**

- Do this first: Row 1 — call the librarian or counselor at the nearest middle and high school, because one call can surface a school-side change since 2022 and gets you the contact you need to reach teens who stopped coming.
- Switch to Row 3 if the schools won't help or can't reach teens who stopped coming, since talking to teens directly is the step you cannot skip.
